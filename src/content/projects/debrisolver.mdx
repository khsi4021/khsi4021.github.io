### The problem

Conjunction assessment still relies heavily on human analysts. They judge tracking-data quality, interpret the probability of collision (Pc), and design avoidance maneuvers, and the workload grows with every new constellation. My Phase III proposal for the DebriSolver competition aimed to automate the routine cases and send only the ambiguous ones to a human.

Two technical problems shaped the design:

- **Dilution.** A small Pc can mean the encounter is safe, or that the tracking data are too poor to tell. I needed to check what Pc *could* be (the maximum Pc) and how credible the orbit determination is.
- **Heterogeneous data.** Public sources such as CelesTrak SOCRATES carry no covariance at all, so the system has to know which inputs it can trust.

### What I built

1. **Data filtration.** Parses CCSDS conjunction data messages (KVN and XML, including TraCSS fields), the ESA challenge CSV, SOCRATES and Space-Track `cdm_public`. It de-duplicates messages and scores credibility on six sub-scores anchored on NASA CARA's orbit-determination quality checks.
2. **Dataset.** One 114-feature learning-table schema, asserted on both training and live inference.
3. **ML risk model.** An XGBoost classifier and value regressor, anchored on the latest risk, aware of the ESA test set's class-prior shift, and explained with SHAP.
4. **Maneuver.** A minimum-ΔV impulse from MATLAB `fmincon` with an analytic warm start, verified by two-body + J2 propagation and Monte Carlo.

A decision layer combines these into **NO_ACTION · MONITOR · REVIEW · MANEUVER** and writes one JSON operator-log line per event, with its reasons.

### Probability of collision

In the encounter plane (ẑ along the relative velocity, x̂ along the miss), Pc is the integral of the combined position uncertainty over the hard-body disk:

<Equation tex={String.raw`P_c = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{|C|}}\iint_{\|\mathbf{r}\| \le R} \exp\!\left[-\tfrac12(\mathbf{r}-\boldsymbol{\mu})^{\mathsf T} C^{-1}(\mathbf{r}-\boldsymbol{\mu})\right] dA`} />

I reduce it exactly to one dimension in the covariance's principal frame. The major axis is integrated analytically as a difference of normal CDFs, evaluated on the tail side for accuracy. The minor axis is integrated with the substitution x = R sin θ, using break points where the chord changes regime and composite Gauss–Legendre quadrature. The same algorithm runs in Python (pipeline) and MATLAB (maneuvers); the two agree to 2.2 × 10⁻¹³.

**Dilution.** Scaling the covariance by k² and maximising Pc(k) gives the worst case. An event with k* < 1 is in the dilution region. I don't auto-clear it if its maximum Pc reaches the action threshold. Instead it goes to REVIEW with the reason "request better tracking data". That is a deliberate departure from NASA CARA's published stance, explained in the report.

### Maneuver design

An impulse Δv in the primary's RTN frame at time τ before TCA moves the miss vector by MΔv, where M = −PΦ<sub>rv</sub>(τ)Q. Φ is the Clohessy–Wiltshire map for near-circular orbits, or a two-body + J2 variational STM otherwise. The optimisation is

<Equation tex={String.raw`\min_{\Delta\mathbf v}\ \|\Delta\mathbf v\| \quad\text{s.t.}\quad P_c(\boldsymbol\mu + M\,\Delta\mathbf v,\ C_{2D},\ R) \le 3.2\times10^{-6}`} />

solved with `fmincon` (SQP) in mm/s for conditioning. The warm start is the eigenvector of MᵀC<sub>2D</sub>⁻¹M with the largest eigenvalue, which is the direction of maximum Mahalanobis displacement per unit Δv. `MultiStart` confirmed the optimum in 15 of 15 checked cases. Every plan is then **propagated** with both objects (two-body + J2, `ode113`, RelTol 10⁻¹²), the TCA is re-found, and Pc is recomputed. For 11 of 567 large burns the linear map fell short, so I re-optimised them with the propagated Pc as the constraint.

### ML protocol

I follow the ESA Collision Avoidance Challenge protocol exactly: 8,293 training and 2,167 test events, scored by L = MSE<sub>HR</sub>/F2. I reproduced the latest-risk baseline (LRP) first, to L = 0.6940 against the paper's 0.694. The test set is deliberately label-shifted, so the deployment prior is estimated from the *unlabelled* test inputs by EM. Hyper-parameters were chosen by grouped cross-validation on the training set only, and the model was evaluated once on test.

<TabPanel id="reflection">

### Limitations

- **The ML model doesn't beat the baseline.** It's tied within noise, and reported as a FAIL.
- **Rectilinear 2D Pc.** I check the short-encounter assumption with Hall–Baars–Casali usage indicators. Flagged events go to REVIEW rather than being computed with a 3D method.
- **ESA covariance-frame approximation.** ESA rows have no absolute states, so the chaser covariance is rotated with an approximate circular target state. The agreement is a median 0.0025 decades, but single events can be offset.
- **Public data are demonstration only.** SOCRATES and Space-Track `cdm_public` carry no covariance, and public TLEs are not adequate for operational conjunction assessment.
- **Secondary conjunctions are only checked within the input set.** Operationally, the maneuvered ephemeris must be screened against the full catalogue.

### Next steps

Screen maneuvered ephemerides against the full catalogue, call `Pc3D_Hall` or Monte Carlo automatically for usage-violation events, re-split the ESA data by mission to retest the regressor honestly, and calibrate the credibility weights against operator-labelled data.

### What I learned

**Validate every stage against something independent before trusting the next one.** The Pc code had to match CARA before any ML touched it, and the baseline had to reproduce L = 0.694 before I trained a model. **Pre-register** what counts as success. Without that, I would have "won" with a variant chosen after seeing the test set.
