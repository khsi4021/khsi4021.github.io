This was a six-person team project in ASEN 1400 Gateway to Space. We built a suborbital payload to test regenerative power generation: using Peltier coolers and the temperature gradients found in space to produce electrical power. The payload reached the Kármán line, flying on the same launch day as the rest of the course's payloads.

On the team, I worked through a predict, test, fly and compare loop.

1. **Payload.** I worked on the design and development of the payload around the Peltier coolers.
2. **Prediction.** I ran a CFD heat-transfer simulation of the payload to get an estimated reading for the regenerative power it would generate.
3. **Ground test.** Before launch I ran vacuum-chamber tests with liquid-nitrogen cooling, to reproduce space thermal conditions and characterise the payload's power generation.
4. **Flight comparison.** After the flight I overlaid the vacuum-chamber results and the simulation estimate on the post-flight telemetry, to check the initial predictions and to see how the system performed under actual flight conditions.

The comparison is the part that carries over to the rest of my work: the simulation estimate was checked against both ground-test and flight measurements.
