### The problem

The capstone RFP calls for a fixed-wing electric UAV with a 55 lb MTOW. It must carry two 1U CubeSats (8.8 lb) at 125 mph for an hour, with takeoff, landing and stall evaluated at 8,000 ft density altitude. At a fixed MTOW, **battery is whatever weight is left over**, so the empty-weight estimate decides whether the mission closes at all. The two models available to us disagreed badly. The toolbox's Raymer statistical fit gave W<sub>e</sub>/W<sub>0</sub> = 0.59 (infeasible), while an uncalibrated component buildup gave 0.35 (comfortably feasible).

### What I did

The course professors gave us the AES Aircraft Design Toolbox, a MATLAB sizing code, and our job was to modify and extend it for our aircraft. My changes:

- **Empty-weight model.** I graded published sources, built a survey dataset of electric fixed-wing UAVs, fit a new regression, and benchmarked it **blind** against an aircraft held out of the fit.
- **Flap model.** I added single-slotted flap increments (Raymer Ch. 12) and wired them into the requirement and mission tables, so CL<sub>max</sub> comes from geometry instead of hand-typed values.
- **Design-space sweeps.** I wrote a sweep over wing area, aspect ratio, motor power, cruise speed and endurance (11,340 designs), then a geometry trade at the design mission.
- **Design mission, mission envelope and sensitivity.** I set up the design mission (125 mph for 36 min, then loiter to 60 min), wrote a tool that maps every cruise/loiter split the battery allows at MTOW, and ranked which assumptions drive range.

### Where the empty-weight model enters

The toolbox converges gross weight from the fixed payload, the battery mass fraction (BMF) and the empty-weight fraction:

<Equation tex={String.raw`W_0 = \frac{W_\text{pay}}{1 - \text{BMF} - W_e/W_0}`} />

At a fixed MTOW, every bit of empty-weight error comes straight out of the battery. My sensitivity study put the elasticity of range to W<sub>e</sub>/W<sub>0</sub> at **−3.09**, the largest of any parameter.

### A survey-based empty-weight model

Datasheets for electric UAVs give MTOW and payload but not battery mass, so only (W<sub>e</sub> + W<sub>batt</sub>)/W<sub>0</sub> is known. The selected model (Fit B) estimates each aircraft's battery from what it actually flies. Cruise power is drag times speed, so

<Equation tex={String.raw`E_i = k\,W_i V_i t_i, \qquad k = \frac{640\ \text{Wh}}{17.7\ \text{kg}\times 22\ \text{m/s}\times 1.83\ \text{h}}`} />

with k anchored on the Penguin BE's published figures and pack energy density chosen by endurance class (145 / 200 / 250 Wh/kg). Then

<Equation tex={String.raw`\log\frac{W_e}{W_0} = \log a + c_1 \log W_0`} />

is fit by least squares to 10 battery-electric UAVs of 9–45 kg. That gives a = 0.9890 and c₁ = −0.1787, with a leave-one-out error of 14.5 %. The Penguin BE is **kept out of the fit** as the validation point. With only 10 aircraft (R² = 0.28) the slope is poorly determined, so the model is used for its value at our weight, 0.483, carried as 0.50 ± 0.05.

Each source got a reliability grade (from A, measured hardware, to D, a marketing figure) and a fit grade (relevance to a 9–36 kg battery-electric fixed-wing aircraft). Calibration and validation data were kept separate.

### High-lift

With single-slotted flaps over 60 % of the wing area (cf/c 0.25, 15° takeoff / 40° landing), the model gives CL<sub>max</sub> = 1.03 clean, 1.29 for takeoff and 1.72 for landing. That replaced the CL<sub>max</sub> of 2.0–2.2 assumed in earlier work, and the allowable stall wing loading dropped from 8.95 to 6.99 psf, a 22 % tightening.

Solving for the flapped-area fraction needed to reach CL<sub>max</sub> 2.2 with single-slotted flaps gives S<sub>wf</sub>/S<sub>ref</sub> = 1.018, more than the whole wing. Even at a generous 70 % flapped-area fraction, single-slotted flaps reach 1.83. Reaching 2.2 needs a double-slotted Fowler system, which is likely outside the budget.

### Weight-model benchmark (blind)

Each model predicts the Penguin BE's empty weight at its 21.5 kg MTOW, against a target of 10.49 kg. The proposed pass band is ±15 %.

| Model | Predicted W<sub>e</sub> | Error | Pass |
|---|---|---|---|
| Raymer `UAV_Prop` | 13.58 kg | +29.4 % | <span class="fail">no</span> |
| Raymer `Homebuilt_Composite_Prop` | 15.80 kg | +50.6 % | <span class="fail">no</span> |
| Survey Fit A (flat battery share) | 12.72 kg | +21.2 % | <span class="fail">no</span> |
| **Survey Fit B (selected)** | **10.67 kg** | **+1.7 %** | <span class="pass">yes</span> |
| Component buildup, uncalibrated | 8.25 kg | −21.3 % | <span class="fail">no</span> |

### What the sweeps found

The first sweep covered 11,340 designs (S 3–12 ft², AR 4–12, 4–6 hp, 70–160 mph, 0.15–1.5 h), run before the flap model and the design mission existed. Energy closure was the main filter:

| Filter | Survivors | Removed |
|---|---|---|
| Energy closure | 2,265 / 11,340 | 9,075 |
| Stall | 1,549 | 716 |
| Landing (μ = 0.4) | 1,549 | 0 |
| Ceiling | 1,537 | 12 |
| MTOW 55 lb | 1,134 | 403 |

One constraint turned out to be artificial. Raymer's landing friction coefficient of 0.2 is the *wet*-surface value, while dry pavement with working brakes is 0.4–0.5. Correcting it took landing from removing 148 designs to removing none.

Mission envelope at MTOW: every cruise/loiter split the leftover battery can fly. With the configuration's (provisional) battery assumption, a 60-minute flight closes with up to about 44 minutes at 125 mph. With the design-matrix baseline battery, it's about 15 minutes.

### Sensitivity ranking

Selected entries: elasticity of range at MTOW, from ±10 % central differences at 125 mph.

| Parameter | Elasticity |
|---|---|
| Empty-weight fraction | **−3.09** |
| MTOW limit | +1.64 |
| Prop efficiency | +1.40 |
| Battery specific energy | +1.24 |
| Cruise speed | −1.11 |
| Parasite drag | −0.86 |
| Motor power | **0.00** |

Prop efficiency outranks battery specific energy because it appears twice: in the Breguet term and in climb power.

<TabPanel id="reflection">

### Limitations

- **The regression rests on 10 aircraft,** with a datasheet-derived battery estimate that assumes every aircraft is as efficient as the Penguin.
- **The sensitivity ranking is local** (one point, ±10 %) and was run before the flap and survey models existed. It needs re-running on the current setup before it's quoted for the final design. A Latin-hypercube study would give global sensitivity.
- **Batteries are the hard limit.** Flying the whole threshold hour at 125 mph (125 mi) would need ~530 Wh/kg cells, and the 225 mi objective ~1000 Wh/kg. Today's best silicon-anode cells are about 450 Wh/kg.
- **The 15° takeoff flap deflection is unoptimised.** CL<sub>max</sub> appears in the denominator of the takeoff constraint while drag grows with deflection, so there is an interior optimum still to find.

### Next steps

Calibrate the component buildup against weighed-hardware data and re-run the blind benchmark, sweep the takeoff flap deflection, and re-run the sensitivity study on the current configuration.

### What I learned

When two models disagree by a factor of 1.7 on the number that decides feasibility, the fix is evidence, not averaging. Grading every source, keeping a held-out benchmark and refusing to tune to it turned an argument into a decision the team could defend at the design review.
