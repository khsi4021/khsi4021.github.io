The problem

Surface roughness can trip a hypersonic boundary layer to turbulence, and turbulence multiplies wall heating. The goal was to predict where that happens on a blunt body: compute the laminar heating, then find the angle where a published roughness correlation says the layer trips.

A missing term

At Pr = 1, total enthalpy must satisfy (CS′)′ + fS′ = 0 for any β. The printed energy equation satisfies it only when β·Me = 0. Restoring the pressure-gradient work term:

(C g′)′ + Pr f g′ = −Pr C(γ−1)Me² f″² − Pr β(γ−1)Me² f′(f′² − g)

With it, the identity holds to 10⁻⁶ and the 30° heat flux moves from 4.15 to 3.55 W/cm², against ≈ 3.6 in the DNS.
