Heating and transition on a blunt body at Mach 6

Where does roughness trip a hypersonic boundary layer? A laminar heating solver, a published transition criterion, and a missing term found along the way.

OverviewTechnicalResultsReflectionCode

The problem

Surface roughness can trip a hypersonic boundary layer to turbulence, and turbulence multiplies wall heating. The aim was to predict where that happens on a blunt body: compute the laminar heating along the surface, then find the angle where a published correlation says the layer trips.

I set the case to the DNS configuration of a published Mach-6 study, so every number could be checked against its results.

A missing term

Marched downstream, the heat flux fell only 6 % over 45°, against about 20 % in the DNS. At Pr = 1, total enthalpy must satisfy (CS′)′ + fS′ = 0 for any β, and the printed energy equation only does when β·Me = 0. It omits the pressure-gradient work term.

(C g′)′ + Pr f g′ = −Pr C(γ−1)Me² f″² − Pr β(γ−1)Me² f′(f′² − g)

With the term restored, the identity holds to 10⁻⁶ and the 30° heat flux moves from 4.15 to 3.55 W/cm², against ≈ 3.6 in the DNS.
